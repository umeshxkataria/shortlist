Asked about Dextr by name

ChatGPT knows Dextr well once someone types the name. It described Daisy, Alfred and Manny, listed OPERA Cloud, Mews and Cloudbeds integrations, and repeated case-study results from Dextr's own site. It still told the buyer to pilot before committing, for two reasons:

  • Only two reviews on Hotel Tech Report, which it called too few to judge.
  • No public pricing, so ROI is hard to estimate before a sales call.

Your content works for people who already know you. Category questions are where new buyers start, and that is where Dextr is missing.

What to fix first

  1. Get properties reviewing you on Hotel Tech Report

    It was cited in half the answers, and ChatGPT used your two reviews as a reason for caution. Dextr already works with hundreds of properties, so asking 20 of them for a review is the cheapest fix on this list.

  2. Publish your own comparison pages

    Conduit's website was cited in five of the eight answers, including ones where Conduit itself was recommended. Buyers ask "alternatives to Canary" and "best AI voice agent for hotels", and ChatGPT answers from pages written to those questions. Dextr needs honest versions of them, with tables.

  3. Write one page per franchise brand

    When asked about Best Western and Wyndham, ChatGPT gave brand-specific advice and put Canary first, citing Wyndham's rollout of it. Dextr serves Hilton, Wyndham, Best Western and IHG franchisees. A page per brand covering systems, approvals and results gives the engine something to cite.

  4. Show a pricing range

    "Pricing isn't public" was the second reason ChatGPT gave for hesitating. A starting price or a per-property range removes it.

  5. Get into the roundups engines already read

    Third-party lists like roommaster.com and Hotel Tech Report's category pages fed several answers. Pitching inclusion there puts Dextr inside the sources, not just on its own site.

  6. Use the buyer's words on your pages

    The questions say "AI voice agent for hotel reservations", "after-hours calls", "staff rostering" and "upsells". Matching that language on the pages for Daisy, Manny and Cosmo makes them easier to match to these questions.
